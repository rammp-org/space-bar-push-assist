Both cell nuts are finger-tight on the bare 8 mm nut-driver bit (about 0.1-0.2 N.m), settled overnight on the bench, re-snugged, then locked with a dab of hot glue, all before the stops are set. The washers are printed (below), 2.4 mm thick: each bridges the 5.5 mm stud slot under its nut (11 MPa at 1.2 x FS pull, row a7R washer); no bought washers.

## Load cell: 20 kg JHD puck (chosen by Alex, 2026-10-27)

**Two 20 kg JHD tension/compression pucks** (Ø25.4 x 12.7 body, M5 studs 12.15 long). The unit is not meant for hard shoves: with the stops at 1.2 x FS a 600 N shove would reach 1.44-1.92 x FS, so keep pushes gentle. The 50 kg puck (same body and studs) drops in with no CAD change if that ever changes. Note the cell's rated output (mV/V) from its label or calibration sheet: the firmware's scale needs it (README).

## Amplifiers: SparkFun Qwiic Scale (NAU7802, SEN-15242), no soldering iron and no blade

Two SparkFun Qwiic Scale boards (a6 review #2: the Adafruit HX711's logic header shipped loose). Nothing on them needs an iron or a small screwdriver: the cell's four wires go into a push-lever spring terminal (press the lever with a fingertip or the tip of a hex key, push the stripped wire in, let go), and the logic side is a Qwiic (JST SH) socket. Per board, one Qwiic-to-female-socket cable (they come long; cut to length) (SparkFun PRT-14988 or Adafruit 4397) to the ESP32 header. The NAU7802 has a fixed I2C address, so the two boards go on the ESP32-P4's two I2C ports. Four Ø3.3 holes on a 17.8 x 25.4 mm pattern (measured on a board). Each amp sits on four 6 mm drill-to-fit pads (Ø10, solid, a centre dimple, no printed pilot). Set the board on them and drill the Ø2.5 pilots through its holes, 9 mm deep; a hole up to 1.5 mm off the modelled pattern still leaves 1.5 mm of wall round the pilot. Then four of the kit's M3 x 10 self-tappers per board.

**ESP32 header: measure before ordering the cables.** The Qwiic-to-female-socket cables need male header pins on the Waveshare ESP32-P4 board's GPIO header. That the board ships with them fitted is not verified; if it does not, buy Qwiic-to-male-pin cables (or a Qwiic breakout) instead, still no soldering on our side. The ESP32's own four standoffs keep printed pilots (its hole pattern is from Waveshare's drawing).

## Printed parts
